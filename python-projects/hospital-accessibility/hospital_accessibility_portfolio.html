Overview

How well-served are India’s major cities for healthcare access? This project downloads real OpenStreetMap data to count hospitals, clinics, pharmacies and healthcare facilities within a 5 km radius of each city centre. An accessibility score (0–100) is computed based on facility density, with a saturation target of 800 facilities. The analysis covers 12 cities from Mumbai to Bhopal, revealing stark differences in healthcare infrastructure across urban India.

Project Details

  • Organization: Personal Research
  • Tools Used: OSMnx, GeoPandas, Matplotlib, Shapely
  • Platform: Google Colab
  • Data Source: OpenStreetMap contributors
  • Radius: 5 km from city centre
  • Facility Types: Hospital, Clinic, Pharmacy, Doctors, Dentist, Health Centre, Healthcare (OSM tag)
  • Saturation target: 800 facilities = score 100

Pipeline

01
Fetch Network
Download full street graph (walk + drive) within 5 km using OSMnx
02
Fetch Facilities
Pull hospitals, clinics, pharmacies via OSMnx features API
03
Count
Count facilities whose centroid falls within the 5 km radius
04
Score
Linear score: count / 800 × 100, capped at 100

Tech Stack

OSMnx GeoPandas Shapely Matplotlib NetworkX Google Colab Python 3

Insights

Bengaluru: 100.0 (Excellent)

India's tech capital, exceptionally dense healthcare network with 1138 facilities, the highest of all cities analysed. MG Road area saturates every healthcare metric.

Hyderabad: 85.5 (Excellent)

Strong healthcare infrastructure driven by HITEC City's growth. 684 facilities reflect a well-distributed mix of corporate hospitals and local clinics across the city.

Mumbai: 76.0 (Excellent)

India's financial capital shows robust healthcare coverage with 608 facilities. The linear coastal geography concentrates services in accessible corridors.

Ahmedabad: 75.1 (Excellent)

Well-served city with 601 facilities reflecting Gujarat's investment in urban healthcare. Even distribution across old city and new western sectors.

Pune: 73.0 (Moderate)

Pune's rapidly growing healthcare sector shows 584 facilities. The university town character brings strong clinic and specialist density near the city centre.

Chennai: 65.1 (Moderate)

Established medical tourism hub with 521 facilities. Anna Salai corridor is dotted with specialist hospitals serving both local and international patients.

Jaipur: 64.1 (Moderate)

Pink City shows surprisingly strong healthcare coverage, 513 facilities reflecting investment in medical infrastructure alongside its tourism economy.

Kolkata: 34.1 (Low)

Historic city shows moderate coverage with 273 facilities. The older urban fabric and OSM data gaps likely understate the real density in tightly-packed central neighbourhoods.
