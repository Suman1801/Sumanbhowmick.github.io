Overview

How has India changed over 60 years of Census data? This project animates the relationship between literacy rate and urbanisation for every Indian state from 1951 to 2011. Each bubble represents one state — its size shows population, its colour shows region, and its position moves decade by decade, revealing stark divergences between Kerala and Bihar, between coastal metros and landlocked heartlands.

💡 Key Insights

Kerala — The Outlier

From 1951, Kerala sits far to the right of every other state on literacy. By 2011 it reaches 94% literacy — near-universal — while simultaneously achieving the highest sex ratio (1084 females per 1000 males). The Kerala model of education-first development is visible in every frame.

Bihar — Stuck Bottom-Left

Through all 7 decades, Bihar barely moves. Starting at 13.5% literacy and 6.4% urban in 1951, it reaches only 61.8% and 11.3% by 2011. Despite having India's third-largest population, it remains one of the least urbanised states — a stark outlier in every frame.

Maharashtra — Steady Climber

Maharashtra shows the most consistent rightward and upward movement of any large state. Starting at 28.8% urban in 1951 (already high due to Mumbai), it reaches 45.2% by 2011 while literacy climbs from 27.9% to 82.3%.

Northeast — Literacy Without Urbanisation

States like Mizoram and Nagaland achieve high literacy rates comparable to Kerala but remain overwhelmingly rural. This unique pattern — educated but not urban — reflects a distinctly different development path from the rest of India.

Note: Some states have missing literacy data for 1951 (notably Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Punjab) due to reorganisation of state boundaries after independence. These states appear from 1961 onwards in the animation.

🕐 Decade by Decade

1951
India newly independent. National literacy at 18.3%, urban population just 17.3%. Most states clustered bottom-left. Kerala already an outlier at 47% literacy.
1961
First decade of Five-Year Plans. Literacy climbs to 28.3%. South Indian states begin separating from the pack. States reorganised — AP, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu now appear.
1971
Green Revolution transforms rural north but urbanisation stays low. Kerala literacy at 69.8% — 35 percentage points ahead of national average. Delhi emerges as most urban state.
1981
Emergency era industrialisation. Maharashtra and Gujarat bubbles move right sharply. National literacy crosses 43%. North-south divide becomes pronounced.
1991
Economic liberalisation begins. IT boom begins seeding Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Literacy crosses 52%. The two-speed India — fast-growing south vs lagging north — is clearly visible.
2001
Post-liberalisation decade. Urban migration accelerates. Bihar and UP remain stubbornly bottom-left despite absolute population growth. Kerala approaches 91% literacy.
2011
India at 1.21 billion. National literacy 73%, urban 31.2%. Kerala at 94% literacy. Delhi 97.5% urban. The bubble chart spreads widest here — greatest divergence between states in history.

Smooth-step interpolation and glow effect — the key visual techniques:

# Smooth-step easing — bubbles accelerate then decelerate
def smooth_step(t):
    return t * t * (3 - 2 * t)

# Interpolate all state values between two census years
for ti in range(N_INTERP):
    t    = (ti + 1) / (N_INTERP + 1)
    ease = smooth_step(t)
    interp["Literacy"]  = cur * (1-ease) + nxt * ease
    interp["Urban_Pct"] = cur * (1-ease) + nxt * ease

# Glow ring — outer bubble at 1.6x size, low alpha
ax.scatter(x, y, s=size * 1.6, c=color,
           alpha=0.12, edgecolors="none", zorder=2)
# Main bubble on top
ax.scatter(x, y, s=size, c=color,
           alpha=0.80, edgecolors="white",
           linewidths=0.6, zorder=3)
