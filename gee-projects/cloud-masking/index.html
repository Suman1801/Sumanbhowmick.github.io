Overview

This project evaluates two widely-used Sentinel-2 cloud masking approaches, QA60 bitmasking and CloudScore+ (cloud probability based), to understand their trade-offs and impact on downstream remote sensing analysis.

Using Google Earth Engine, both methods are applied over Barasat, India and compared across cloud-free coverage, handling of thin clouds and haze, visual composite quality, and practical guidance on threshold tuning.

⚖ Method Comparison

Both approaches applied to the same Sentinel-2 scenes over Barasat, India.

QA60 Bitmasking

  • Source: Sentinel-2 L1C QA60 band
  • Bit 10: Opaque cloud flag
  • Bit 11: Cirrus cloud flag
  • Output: Binary mask, cloud or clear, no probability
  • Speed: Very fast, no additional model required
  • Weakness: Can leave thin clouds and haze unmasked
  • Best for: Quick processing, rough mapping, broad overviews

CloudScore+

  • Source: Per-pixel cloud probability score (0 to 1)
  • Threshold: Typically 40 to 60% probability cutoff
  • Output: Continuous probability with user-defined threshold
  • Speed: Slower, requires probability layer computation
  • Strength: Handles thin clouds, haze, and shadow more effectively
  • Best for: Index computation, change detection, high-precision work

💡 Insights

Coverage vs. Cleanliness

QA60 is fast and conservative but regularly leaves thin clouds and haze in the composite. CloudScore+ with a well-chosen threshold produces visually cleaner results but removes slightly more valid pixels in the process.

Threshold Tuning Matters

CloudScore+ probability thresholds of 40 to 60% change outcomes noticeably. Lower thresholds retain more surface area but risk residual clouds; higher thresholds remove more haze but may over-mask bright surfaces like sand, concrete, or snow.

Use Case Driven Choice

For spectral indices (NDVI, NDWI) or change detection, CloudScore+ tends to be more reliable due to reduced haze contamination. For quick overviews or rough-classification mapping, QA60 is a sufficient and efficient baseline.

Reproducibility

Both pipelines were implemented as fully commented GEE JavaScript scripts. Clear documentation of threshold choices and export parameters ensures results can be reproduced or adapted for other study areas with minimal effort.

✅ Conclusion

CloudScore+ generally provides cleaner, more flexible cloud masking at the cost of requiring threshold calibration. Its ability to handle thin clouds and haze makes it the stronger choice for analytical workflows where surface reflectance accuracy is critical.

QA60 remains a reliable and computationally simple baseline. When speed or simplicity matters, or when the study area has predominantly dense cloud cover where both methods perform similarly, it is a perfectly reasonable choice.

Ultimately, the decision should be guided by project goals, tolerance for omission and commission errors, the nature of the cloud cover in the region of interest, and the time available for threshold calibration and QA validation.
