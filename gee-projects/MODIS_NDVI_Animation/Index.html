Overview

This project visualises the Green Wave, the seasonal cycle of vegetation growth and senescence across the Indian subcontinent. By processing over 20 years of MODIS data, the script creates a synthetic typical year (climatology) that demonstrates long-term phenological patterns stripped of year-specific anomalies.

Using Google Earth Engine, a custom Day-of-Year (DOY) joining methodology was implemented to reduce noise from cloud cover and sensor artifacts, resulting in a smooth, high-fidelity animation of India's agricultural and natural land-cover cycles across all 23 MODIS 16-day compositing windows.

Objectives

  • Automate the retrieval and filtering of MODIS Terra NDVI 16-day composites over India.
  • Implement a DOY-based climatology to visualise long-term average seasonal vegetation shifts.
  • Export a high-resolution GIF animation suitable for environmental reporting and communication.

Pipeline

01
Metadata Tagging
Extract the relative day-of-year for every image in the MODIS Terra MOD13A2 archive
02
Temporal Joining
Group 20+ years of satellite observations into 23 distinct 16-day DOY windows using a climatological join
03
Median Reduction
Apply per-pixel median reducers across each DOY window to eliminate year-specific anomalies and sensor artifacts
04
Animation Export
Apply a 17-step NDVI colour ramp and export a looping GIF animation to Google Drive

💡 Insights

Monsoon Influence

The animation clearly depicts the northward progression of the Indian Summer Monsoon and the resulting green-up wave that sweeps from the Western Ghats and the eastern coast inward. The timing and intensity of this signal is consistent with long-term climatological records.

Agricultural Intensity

Double-cropping patterns in the Indo-Gangetic Plain are visible as secondary green-up pulses in the post-monsoon Rabi season. This signal is distinct from the monsoon-driven Kharif green-up and highlights the density of irrigated agriculture across northern India.

Climatological Stability

The median reduction approach effectively filters out year-specific droughts, floods, or anomalous monsoon years. The resulting animation represents the long-term typical seasonal behaviour rather than any single year, making it a robust baseline for change detection.

Regional Contrasts

The Thar Desert and Deccan rain-shadow zones remain persistently brown through most of the cycle, contrasting sharply with the evergreen cover of the Western Ghats and the Northeast, which maintain high NDVI values year-round regardless of monsoon timing.

Conclusion

The MODIS NDVI climatology script serves as a powerful baseline for environmental monitoring and phenological research. By compressing over two decades of satellite observations into a single representative annual cycle, it makes complex temporal dynamics immediately interpretable to a broad audience.

The DOY joining and median reduction methodology is transferable to any region or vegetation index and can be adapted for anomaly detection by comparing individual years against this long-term climatological baseline.

This project demonstrates the ability to process large-scale geospatial datasets in Google Earth Engine and transform complex temporal data into intuitive visual products suitable for scientific communication and environmental reporting.
